Climbing red crosses: corrupted relay copies at Billaboo and Blackdown Tableland

Investigation 01 · 27 October 2026 · Billaboo AL (535082, rain 5927) and Blackdown Tableland AL (35186, rain 5910), Emerald network · Open: cause narrowed, not confirmed

  • The crosses above the line aren't rain. Each is a second copy of a real report, damaged as it was relayed. It arrives about a second after a clean copy, with the right count but some upper bits changed.
  • Old ALERT frames have no checksum. Their few fixed check bits catch damage at the start of a frame but not at the end, where the value's top bits travel. So the damage gets through, and almost always reads high.
  • Blackdown Tableland's odd reset is a real wrap plus the same fault. Once the counter restarted from 0, its top bit could be forced to 1, which put copies 204.8 mm above the line.
  • Emerald Radar AL is the first place to look: it's the only repeater both stations go through. Not proven, because Flood-Net's receivers don't hear this network.

A clean copy, then a corrupted one a second later

58 of the 66 corrupted transmissions arrived within two seconds of a clean copy of the same count, 43 of them exactly one second after: about one store-and-forward relay hop. Both receive paths in the export got the same wrong value at the same moment, so the damage happened before they split.

Rain multiplies it but doesn't cause it. About the same share of transmissions came back corrupted in heavy rain (14%, 49 of 341), light rain (16%) and the dry three-hourly reports (9%, 5 of 57). The crosses bunch in storms because the gauge transmits on every tip.

Why the damage gets through, and nearly always upward

Of Billaboo's 66, 32 had one bit forced from 0 to 1 and 10 had two. 14 were the last byte read late, 12 of them by exactly one bit (1,346 became 1,698 three times in twelve minutes on 24 October). Damage early in a frame breaks a 1 0 check, and the frame is dropped; a changed address files the reading under another sensor. So what reaches this sensor was damaged at the end, and it reads high.

Blackdown Tableland: a real wrap, then +1,024 copies

27 of its 28 corrupted transmissions arrived within two seconds of a clean copy, as at Billaboo. Before the wrap the count was over 1,024, so its top bit was already 1 and couldn't be forced: 0 of 12 corrupted copies changed it. After the wrap the top bit was 0, and 11 of 16 were exactly that bit forced to 1, which put copies like 594 → 1,618 back near the old level.

Across the network: errors bunch at the ends of the frame

The first and last bytes are hit hardest, which points to timing as a transmission starts and ends rather than to one faulty data line. D5–D9 flip up and down equally, so nothing is stuck high. Only the top bit goes 0→1 2.6× as often as 1→0, as a late read predicts: it hands that bit the check bit after it, which is always 1. Start-of-frame errors fit a frequency synthesiser (PLL) that hasn't settled. That could be a transmitter keying up too fast: those copies came in on carriers 46 Hz from the clean copy's, against 6–14 Hz elsewhere. Or it could be our own decoder's bit-timing loop still locking during a short preamble.

Where it enters

Billaboo is relayed by Mt Mayde (15 km away) and Emerald Radar (94 km); Blackdown Tableland by Roddas Lookout and Emerald Radar, all on 151.5 MHz. At both stations the corrupted copy is one hop behind the clean one, and Emerald Radar is the only repeater on both paths. Both exports also share one receive path's slow, drifting clock, so they reach the Bureau through the same base station.

That narrows it without convicting anyone: Flood-Net's receivers don't hear this network, so carrier fingerprints can't name the sender.

What we know

Established. These are corrupted relay copies a hop behind a clean one, damaged at the frame's end and almost always high. Blackdown Tableland's wrap is real. Codes DD, PD, ND and AN mark every one of them (3,978 rows checked), so excluding them in Plots (the Tyalgum preset) removes the crosses.

Likely. A timing fault at the end of a relayed frame: read a bit late, or the transmitter dropping before its last byte is done.

Open. Which site; and how much start-of-frame error is a transmitter's PLL, how much our decoder.
