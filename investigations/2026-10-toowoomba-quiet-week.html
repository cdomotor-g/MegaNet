Toowoomba's quiet week: what changed when its repeater went off air

Investigation 02 · 29 October 2026 · Toowoomba AL (540162, repeater on 151.525 MHz) and Hillcrest AL (Wine Glass) (540235, repeater on 151.500 MHz), Brisbane hub · Open: Toowoomba implicated, Hillcrest still to separate

  • Toowoomba's transmitter last spoke at 04:35 on 25 October. Its rain counter had reset to 0 at 03:16, which looks like a power cut or a restart. Its carrier hasn't been heard since.
  • On its channel, corrupted copies fell to a third at that moment: from 53 to 19 per 1,000 decoded, with the same traffic, back where it was in early October. Our receiver's update the day before didn't do it: the rate stayed at 46 until Toowoomba stopped.
  • Toowoomba was sending some of the damage itself. 8.4% of the bursts on its carrier were corrupted copies, about twice the share on the other two repeaters on its channel. Mt Glorious, which relays the same addresses, went from 3.8% to 2.0% once Toowoomba stopped.
  • Hillcrest is a separate fault. Its channel didn't improve when Toowoomba stopped. It got worse, 35 to 47 per 1,000. So Toowoomba isn't behind it.

The test: one channel cleared, the other didn't

On Toowoomba's channel the rate ran at 19 per 1,000 in early October and rose to 58 from 15 October, with no change in traffic (about 1,018 and 1,119 bursts a day). It stayed high through the storm of 22–24 October. When Toowoomba went quiet it dropped to 19, still on about 1,126 bursts a day: 82 corrupted copies in 4,289, against 921 in 17,361 from 15 October up to the silence. That's 0.36 times the earlier rate (95% range 0.29–0.45), too large a drop for chance. 98% of the corrupted copies on this channel were clear: as strong as the clean copy, so wrong when they were sent, not misheard.

What went quiet at 04:35 on 25 October

The tall peak near +560 Hz is Toowoomba. 91% of its 2,638 bursts were addresses in Toowoomba's pass ranges, and all 128 reports from its own gauge came on it. After 04:35 on 25 October, one burst from its ranges arrived anywhere near that offset. The Mt Glorious peak carries addresses only Mt Glorious relays, and it's still there.

Before 15 October almost every one of its three-hourly reports arrived (2 of 64 missed). On the 15th, the day the corruption rose, 5 of 8 went missing, and 10 by the 18th. On 25 October its count read 222, then 0 at 03:16, then 1 to 4 by 04:23 as it rained. Its carrier was last heard at 04:35, 78 minutes after the reset. The one copy of its address since, on 27 October at 18:02, came on Mt Glorious's carrier (+374 Hz) with a count of 655 that doesn't follow Toowoomba's. That one is another gauge, not Toowoomba.

Before and after, side by side

Each window is a stretch of the fortnight. The orange bars stay high through the storm and through our receiver's update, 17 hours before the silence (69 corrupted in 1,499, 46 per 1,000). The update didn't help Hillcrest's channel either.

Toowoomba's own addresses, which now reach us only through the repeaters that share its ranges, went from 87 to 38 per 1,000 (32 in early October). Addresses outside its ranges went from 26 to 10 (8 early), so it was spoiling traffic it doesn't list as well. Either it relays more than its pass ranges say, or its transmissions were colliding with other repeaters' bursts.

From 21 October, when carriers were first recorded, to the silence, 222 corrupted copies came on Toowoomba's carrier: 54% of all those on the channel. Mt Glorious's share fell from 3.8% to 2.0% when Toowoomba stopped. It relays Toowoomba's addresses onward, so some of its damage was Toowoomba's, passed on. The third repeater stayed about where it was (4.2% to 5.0%).

Hillcrest: a fault of its own

89% of the bursts on 151.500, and 90% of its corrupted copies, are addresses Hillcrest passes. That channel ran at 28 per 1,000 in early October and 35 from 15 October. It went up, not down, to 47 after Toowoomba stopped, on less traffic (about 1,641 of Hillcrest's bursts a day, against 2,532 in early October). Whatever corrupts Hillcrest's traffic, Toowoomba isn't part of it.

Hillcrest and Constitution Hill pass the same ranges on the same channel. 84% of what we hear on 151.500 arrives on one carrier, which also brings Hillcrest's own gauge. So the carrier can't yet say which of the two does the damage. Flood-Net's transmitter table, as judged at 05:13 on 29 October, puts 109 corrupted copies on Hillcrest against 188 bursts (58%), and 26% on Toowoomba, against a typical 0.39%. Those shares are too high. The table divides by the bursts it pinned to the repeater itself, not everything on its carrier. It also gave most of Toowoomba's carrier to Mt Glorious, which relays the same addresses. Measured on the whole carrier, Toowoomba's share is 8.4%.

What we know

Established. Toowoomba's transmitter stopped at 04:35 on 25 October. On its channel, corrupted copies fell from 53 to 19 per 1,000 at that moment, on the same traffic, and our receiver's update didn't cause it. Hillcrest's channel didn't improve.

Likely. Toowoomba was failing for ten days before it died, and sending corrupted copies the whole time. Some went out again through Mt Glorious. A counter reset, then silence 78 minutes later in rain, points at power: battery, charger or supply.

Open. What failed on 15 October. Whether Hillcrest or Constitution Hill corrupts 151.500.

Next

  1. Visit Toowoomba AL: power first (battery, charger, solar, supply), then the radio. When it's back, watch Figure 1: if the orange line climbs again, it's confirmed.
  2. Run the same test on Hillcrest. Ask the maintainer when Hillcrest or Constitution Hill will next be off air, and compare 151.500 before and after.
  3. Fix the transmitter table so it uses the whole carrier as the denominator, and so a repeater can't be credited with another's carrier. The Bit-Flip Sources tab and the bulletins read from it.

Method: Flood-Net's receiver, legacy ALERT bursts on 151.500 and 151.525 MHz, 7–28 October (Brisbane time). A corrupted copy is one Flood-Net's checks found: a burst one or two bits from a real one, on the same receiver within five seconds. Rates are corrupted copies over bursts decoded. An address belongs to Toowoomba or Hillcrest if it is in that repeater's pass ranges in the station register. The receiver's other channels changed during the fortnight, so they are left out. Words used here.

Corrupted copy
A second copy of a real report, heard within five seconds of it, with one or two of its address or value bits changed.
Per 1,000 decoded
Corrupted copies for every 1,000 bursts our receiver decoded on that channel, all addresses together.
Carrier offset
How far a transmitter's carrier sits from the channel's centre, as our receiver measures it. Every crystal is off by its own amount, so the offset tells apart transmitters that share a channel.
Pass range
The block of addresses a repeater is set to relay. Toowoomba passes 4000–4099, 6526–6527 and its own gauge, 6598.
Clear
A corrupted copy as strong as the clean one, within 6 dB: the transmitter sent wrong bits, rather than our receiver mishearing a weak one.
Three-hourly report
Toowoomba's gauge sends its count at two minutes past 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20 and 23 o'clock, rain or not.
